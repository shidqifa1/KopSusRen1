# Anti-Slop UI Audit 001

Project: Baristack App  
Mode: AFTER  
Date: 2026-09-21  
Scope: `index.html`, `styles.css`, public login screen at `https://baristack.my.id/`, mobile viewport 390 x 844, desktop viewport 1366 x 768, and existing automated checks.  
Change policy: audit only. No UI source code was changed.

## Design read

The current UI uses a warm coffee-shop palette, DM Serif Display for identity, Plus Jakarta Sans for operational content, compact cards, and a mobile-first drawer. That direction is visually coherent, but it is inferred from the implementation because no `DESIGN.md`, brand guide, or written design rationale exists.

Observed dials: ENERGY 1, RHYTHM 1, MOTION 1.

## Numbered findings

1. **HIGH, R-32: Global focus visibility is removed.** Reason: `styles.css:42-44` applies `outline: none` to every focused element, while only inputs, selects, and textareas receive a replacement focus treatment at `styles.css:345-349`; buttons, tabs, navigation, and modal controls have no reliable visible keyboard focus.

2. **HIGH, R-32 and R-26: Main navigation is pointer-only.** Reason: the destinations at `index.html:99-109` are clickable `div` elements without native button or link semantics, `tabindex`, or keyboard handlers, so they cannot be reached and activated consistently with Tab, Enter, or Space.

3. **HIGH, R-32 and R-26: Modals are not accessible dialogs.** Reason: the three modal backdrops at `index.html:427-490` have no dialog role, accessible labelling, focus trap, focus restoration, or Escape-key close behavior, and their 32 x 32 close controls are also below the mobile tap-target minimum.

4. **HIGH, R-03: Browser zoom is disabled and viewport rules conflict.** Reason: `index.html:16` blocks user scaling and a second viewport declaration appears at `index.html:25`, which prevents users with low vision from zooming and makes mobile behavior browser-dependent.

5. **HIGH, R-03: Several recurring controls are smaller than 44 x 44 px.** Reason: `.modal-close` is 32 x 32, `.po-mode-tabs button` is 36 px high, and `.btn-sm` plus `.tab-btn` use compact padding without a 44 px minimum, making frequent actions error-prone on touch screens.

6. **HIGH, R-25: Multiple text combinations fail WCAG AA contrast.** Reason: `--abu2` is used extensively at 10 to 13 px but measures only 3.00:1 on `--putih` and 2.69:1 on `--krem`, while white text on the orange warning toast measures 2.85:1; normal text requires at least 4.5:1.

7. **HIGH, R-27: Data views have generic dead-end empty states.** Reason: messages such as “Belum ada data penjualan”, “Tidak ada data”, “Belum ada mutasi kas”, and “Belum ada laporan tersimpan” identify the absence but do not explain why it is empty or give the next action that creates data.

8. **HIGH, R-02: User-facing copy contains an em dash.** Reason: the stock instruction at `index.html:245` uses an em dash even though the rule requires punctuation such as a comma, period, colon, or parentheses.

9. **HIGH, R-03 and R-32: Logged-out users still receive the application UI behind the login overlay.** Reason: the public accessibility snapshot exposed the drawer, dashboard, metrics, chart, and order control before authentication, and at 390 x 844 the document was 915 px tall even though the login content fit, creating unnecessary scrolling and a confusing assistive-technology reading order.

10. **HIGH, R-35: The authenticated interface has no complete recorded click-through.** Reason: the public login screen and source were inspected and all automated checks passed, but every post-login button, tab, modal, form, error path, and mobile breakpoint has not yet been exercised in this audit, so deployment-level UI verification cannot be claimed.

11. **HIGH, R-37: The shipped visual direction is undocumented.** Reason: no `DESIGN.md`, brand guide, or explicit one-line rationale was found for palette, typography, card hierarchy, spacing, icons, or motion, so the existing direction cannot be distinguished from an intentional system versus an accumulated draft.

12. **MEDIUM, R-04: Decorative emoji are used as the primary icon system.** Reason: coffee cups, robots, hearts, boxes, calendars, and other emoji are scattered through branding, navigation, headings, buttons, and status feedback without a documented relevance or a consistent visual language.

## Checks that passed

- The 390 px audit showed no horizontal page overflow.
- The warm brown, green, and cream palette is coherent and avoids generic blue-purple gradients.
- Existing cards and data values are tied to real application content rather than fabricated testimonials or fake metrics.
- The manifest and visible product name consistently use Baristack App.
- `npm run check` passed 9 of 9 suites on 2026-09-21.

## Verification boundary

No credential was entered during this audit. Findings 1 through 9 and 11 through 12 are supported by source inspection and the public rendered state. Finding 10 records the remaining authenticated verification work instead of claiming an unperformed pass.
